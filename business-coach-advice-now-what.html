After the coaching conversation

Business coach advice.
Now what?

Here’s a simple way to turn the advice into a clear action, an owner and a way to see progress.

✳Capture the advice. Connect it to a business priority, choose what to try next, and decide how you’ll know it helped.
01

What are you trying to change?

Describe the situation in your own words. Keep it specific enough to act on.

✦A useful lens: Start with the progress you want to make, not the tool or activity you might use.
02

What advice or evidence do you have?

Capture the useful bit. You can add the source or context if it matters.

✦A useful lens: With JTBD, ask what triggered the need and what progress the person is trying to make.
03

Choose a small next step

Turn the advice into something someone can actually try.

✦A useful lens: Make the action small enough to learn from. Name one owner and the next decision.
04

How will you know it helped?

Use a baseline where you can. Leave targets blank until you have evidence.

✦A useful lens: For an OKR, write one outcome-focused objective, then a few measurable results. Don’t mistake activity for progress.

Illustrative examples

Try a real estate scenario

Use one as a starting point, then edit it to fit your own process and data.

New lead follow-up01

Explore whether qualified buyer enquiries get a timely, personal next step. Check response time and follow-up records before choosing a target.

Campaign momentum02

Explore what the team can learn from enquiry, inspection, buyer follow-up and vendor updates. Compare days on market across similar listings.

Examples are prompts to investigate, not claims about your current process. Days on market is affected by many factors.

Pick a lens if it helps

Optional prompts—not steps you have to complete.

JTBD · understand the need

What happened? What progress is someone trying to make? What are they doing today instead?

OKR · make progress visible

One objective describes the outcome. Key results show how you’ll recognise progress.

Opportunity tree · explore before building

Desired outcome → customer or team opportunities → possible solutions → small tests.

RAID / ROAM · surface delivery conditions

Capture risks, assumptions, issues and dependencies. Then decide whether each is resolved, owned, accepted or mitigated.

Fletch · sharpen the problem

Who needs progress, what triggered the need, what alternatives exist, and what might block a better outcome?
